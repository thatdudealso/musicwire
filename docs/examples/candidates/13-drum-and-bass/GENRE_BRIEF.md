# Drum and bass production brief

**Why this lane:** Drum and bass is a high-energy UK electronic staple that no other candidate covers. House, techno, and big-room are all four-on-the-floor; DnB is breakbeat + Reese bass at ~174 BPM, so the gallery gets a distinct rhythmic family.

Sources (accessed 2026-09-01):

- Wikipedia, "Drum and bass": 160-180 BPM, typically 170-180 since the mid-90s; fast breakbeats, heavy sub bass, synthesizers; bass often sampled/synth rather than electric bass.
- Sample Focus tempo guide: 170-180 BPM, sweet spot 172-176; rolling breakbeats; basslines often half-time against fast drums.
- Beat Kitchen drum programming: basic two-step DnB - kick on step 1 and the and-of-2 (step 10), snare on 2 and 4, 16th hats, ghost snares. Groove from broken kick placement, not swing.
- Beat Kitchen genre landscape: drums define the genre more than anything; Amen-style chopped breaks are historical source material - **do not quote the Amen break**.

## Tempo, key, length

- 174 BPM, 4/4, F-sharp minor (three sharps). Mid-170s hallmark.
- 120 bars = 165.5s.
- Divisions=4.

## Harmony and groove

Loop: F#m | D | E | C# (i - VI - VII - V). Bass moves in half notes while drums race.

Two-step kit (16th grid): kick steps 1 and 10; snare 5 and 13; hats 16ths; ghost snare on 3 and 11 during drops.

## Arrangement arc (120 bars)

1. Bars 1-16: pad + hats (DJ-in).
2. Bars 17-32: bass enters, still no breakbeat.
3. Bars 33-48: first drop (full breakbeat + stab).
4. Bars 49-64: mid-break, kick drops out.
5. Bars 65-96: second drop (peak, extra ghost snares).
6. Bars 97-120: outro strip to hats + bass, cadence on F#.

## Anti-patterns

- Four-on-the-floor (that is house/techno).
- Quoting the Amen break or any sampled break melody.
- Electric-guitar metal mapping.
- Pop verse-chorus as the primary form.
