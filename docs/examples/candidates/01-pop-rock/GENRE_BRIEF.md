# Pop-rock production brief

Internal lane: bright anthemic guitar-pop, punchy drums, big singalong-shaped hooks (5 Seconds of Summer energy class). Original instrumental only. Do not quote any copyrighted melody, riff, or lyric.

Sources (accessed 2026-09-01):

- Lyric Assistant, "How to Write Pop Rock Songs" (2024-09-14): pop-rock anthems sit 90-140 BPM, often 120-140; verse-prechorus-chorus-verse-prechorus-chorus-bridge-final-chorus; guitar uses open chords, palm mutes, and power chords for contrast; drums stay in pocket on verses and push on choruses.
- Open Music Theory, "Form in pop/rock music": verse-chorus form with optional prechorus; choruses are louder, thicker, and more regular than verses (loose-verse / tight-chorus).
- SciSpace PDF, "Rhythmic functions in pop-rock music": explicit beat layer is kick 1+3, snare 2+4, hats on even eighths.

## Tempo, key, length

- 132 BPM, 4/4, A major (three sharps). Anthem window from Lyric Assistant (120-140).
- 88 bars = 160.0s written (`88 * 4 * 60 / 132`). Target 2.5-3:00.
- Divisions=4 so verse palm-mutes and chorus eighth hooks share a grid.

## Harmony and groove

Loop: A | D | F#m | E (I - IV - vi - V). Not the I-V-vi-IV pop-punk staple. Regular 8-bar phrases.

Verse guitar: muted eighths on the root with a fifth on beat 4. Chorus: open A/D/F#m/E power-chord halves so the lead hook can sit on top.

Kick 1+3, snare 2+4, hats eighths. Crash on chorus bar 1.

## Arrangement arc (88 bars)

1. Bars 1-8: intro hook. Lead guitar + hats; kick/snare enter bar 5.
2. Bars 9-24: verse. Distortion palm-mutes, bass, full backbeat; lead tacet after a one-bar answer.
3. Bars 25-32: prechorus. Hats denser, lead climbs.
4. Bars 33-48: chorus. Open power chords, lead hook, crash on 1.
5. Bars 49-56: verse two (half length).
6. Bars 57-72: chorus two.
7. Bars 73-80: bridge. Drop to bass + snare; lead holds a higher cell.
8. Bars 81-88: final chorus tag on A.

## Anti-patterns

- Generic piano substituting for the guitar/kit skeleton.
- Four-on-the-floor house kick.
- Copying any real pop-rock hook (including the named-lane catalog).
- Identical rhythm in verse and chorus.
- Pitched "drum" notes instead of unpitched kit.
